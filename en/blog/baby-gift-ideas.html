What to buy for a new baby: ideas and group gifts

October 8, 2026 · 7 minute read

Choosing a gift for a newborn looks easy, but families often end up with five copies of the same bodysuit and none of the things they actually need. The best gift is one the family really wants and nobody else has bought yet. Here are ideas by budget, and an easy way to buy big gifts together.

Ask the family first

If the family has made a gift list, choosing from it saves time for you and for them. If there is no list, a few questions are enough: which sizes do they like? Would they like contributions towards a big item, such as a pram or a crib? Do they have colour or brand preferences?

Ideas by budget

Small budget

  • A muslin set: used every day as a swaddle, nursing cover and burp cloth
  • Clothes in 6–12 months instead of 0–3 months (the first sizes arrive in plenty)
  • A cloth or board book
  • A baby care kit: nail scissors, brush, thermometer

Medium budget

  • A sleeping bag for the right season
  • A changing bag
  • A baby carrier or sling
  • A play mat

Big budget or group gift

  • A pram or travel system
  • A car seat
  • A bedside crib
  • A high chair (used from around 6 months)

Gifts for mum and the family

In the weeks after the birth, the most useful things are often for the family rather than the baby:

  • A few days of home-cooked meals or a meal delivery
  • Help with cleaning
  • Comfortable nursing pyjamas or a good water bottle for mum
  • A small gift for the big sibling, which means a lot while attention shifts to the new baby

Traditional gifts

In Turkey it is a common tradition to give a newborn a small gold coin. Because gold keeps its value as the child grows up, many families prefer it. Adding a short letter to the baby alongside the gift leaves a memory to be read years later.

How to organise a group gift

An expensive item like a pram is hard for one person to buy. When a group of friends or relatives buys it together, the family gets what they really need and nobody stretches their budget. To organise it:

  1. Choose the item and the target amount. Ask the family which model they want.
  2. Let everyone give as much as they like. Asking for a fixed share can leave some people out.
  3. Make the total visible. A progress bar shows everyone how much is left.
  4. Keep track of who joined. The family will remember everyone when saying thank you.

"Who gave how much?" messages in a group chat quickly get confusing. Keeping the gift list and the group gift on one page removes that confusion.

A few notes for the first visit

  • Ask whether the family is up for a visit; the first weeks are tiring.
  • Keep it short, and postpone if you are unwell.
  • Wash your hands before holding the baby.
  • Bringing a meal is often the most welcome gift of all.
