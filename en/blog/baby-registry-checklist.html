Baby registry checklist: what a newborn really needs

October 8, 2026 · 8 minute read

Shop "must-have" lists are usually far longer than what a baby uses in the first months. We split this list by category into what you need on day one and what can wait. That protects your budget and gives your loved ones a clear list to buy from.

Sleep

A newborn spends most of the day asleep, so the most important purchase is a safe place to sleep. Expert bodies broadly agree: babies should sleep on their back, on a flat and firm mattress, with no pillows, duvets, bumpers or toys, in their own bed but in the parents' room.

  • A crib, bedside bassinet or travel cot (with a firm, well-fitting mattress)
  • 2–3 fitted sheets that sit tightly on the mattress
  • 2–3 sleeping bags or swaddles (lighter or warmer depending on the season)
  • A dim night light for night-time changes
Safety note: Cot bumpers, pillows and thick blankets can increase the risk of suffocation in the first months. Ask your paediatrician about anything related to sleep.

Clothing

Babies grow very fast in the first weeks. Buy very little in newborn size and a bit more in 0–3 months. Clothes are also the most common gift, so keep your own shopping light.

  • 6–8 bodysuits (front-opening ones are much easier in the first weeks)
  • 4–6 sleepsuits or pyjamas
  • 2–3 light cardigans
  • Socks, a thin hat and an outer layer for the season
  • Fragrance-free laundry detergent for baby clothes

Feeding

Breastfeeding, formula or a mix: it is hard to know for sure before the baby arrives. Start with the basics and add the rest in the first weeks as you learn what you need.

  • A nursing pillow and 2 nursing bras
  • Breast pads
  • 2–3 slow-flow bottles and a bottle brush
  • 6–10 burp cloths or muslins
  • A breast pump if needed (consider renting or borrowing first)

Care and bathing

  • A changing mat or a portable changing pad
  • Newborn nappies for the first week and size 1 after that (babies outgrow sizes fast, so don't stock up too much)
  • Wipes, or cotton wool and warm water
  • Nappy cream
  • A baby bath and 2–3 hooded towels
  • Soft nail scissors or a nail file
  • A digital thermometer

Travel and going out

For the first ride home from the hospital, a rear-facing infant car seat is the most important item. Travel systems that bundle a pram and car seat are practical but expensive, which makes them a great group gift.

  • An infant car seat suitable for newborns
  • A pram or pushchair
  • A changing bag
  • Optional: a baby carrier or sling

What can wait

Most families don't need these in the first months, or can buy them when the need comes up:

  • A high chair (solid food usually starts around 6 months)
  • A baby walker, playpen and large toys
  • A baby monitor (many families don't need one while the baby sleeps in their room)
  • Lots of newborn-size clothes
  • A bottle steriliser and formula machine (decide once your feeding routine is clear)

Turning the list into gifts

Once the list is ready, the biggest problem is relatives buying the same thing twice. Everyone asks "What should we get?", but they each ask someone different. Putting the list in one link and sharing it makes things easier for everyone:

  • Each guest reserves an item with "I'll get this", and it shows as reserved on the list.
  • For big gifts like a pram, everyone chips in as much as they like.
  • Relatives can suggest items, and they join the list once you approve them.

Knowing who got what also helps when you send thank-you notes after the birth.
