Letters to the baby: what to write, with examples

October 8, 2026 · 6 minute read

A letter to a baby feels like a small note when you write it. But when the child grows up and reads it, it is proof of how eagerly they were awaited before they were even born. If you don't know what to write, the tips and family examples below will help you start.

In this article

How to write a letter to a baby

  • Introduce yourself. A line like "I'm your aunt Zeynep" reminds the child, years later, who you are.
  • Describe today. What was the weather like while everyone waited, what was the family doing, how did you feel?
  • Leave a wish. What would you like to teach them, or do with them?
  • Keep it short. A few honest sentences are worth more than a long, general text.
  • Add the date. When the letter was written becomes one of the most loved details when it is read.

Examples from the family

From mum

The first time I felt you, I was making tea in the kitchen. I stopped, put my hand on my belly and stayed like that for a while. Since that day every kick of yours says "I'm here". I can't wait to meet you.

Mum

From dad

We changed our minds three times about the colour while painting your room. In the end we went with the one your mum chose, and I suspect that's how it will go from now on. I'm already planning our first bike ride together.

Dad

From grandma

My dear grandchild, I waited for your mum just like this when she was your size. Now I'm waiting for her child, and my heart is beating with the same excitement for the second time. I'm counting the days until I hold you.

Fatma, your grandma

From grandpa

I planted a sapling in the garden for you. It will grow as you grow. One day we'll sit in its shade together and I'll read you this letter.

Your grandpa Ahmet

From an aunt

I'll be the one to read you your first book, promise! And when your mum is cross with you, you can come to me. It stays between us.

Zeynep, your aunt

From a family friend

I've known your mum and dad for years; you're arriving next to the two people who will love you most. Welcome, little one!

Can, a friend of the family

When should the letters be opened?

Some families read the letters right after the birth; others keep them for the first birthday, or for much later, such as the child's 18th birthday. Opening the letters on a chosen day adds its own excitement to that day. Knowing this also helps the writers write more openly.

Keeping the letters

Paper letters can get lost over the years. Collecting them in one place and adding photos from that time creates a memory book you can give the child when they grow up. Recording who wrote each letter, along with how they are related (grandma, aunt, grandpa), introduces the family to the child who reads it years later.
