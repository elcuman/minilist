Hospital bag checklist: mum, baby and partner

October 8, 2026 · 7 minute read

Labour rarely starts exactly as planned. A bag that is already packed lets you focus on one thing on the day. This list is split into four parts: documents, mum, baby and the person coming with you. Print it and tick things off as you go.

When to pack

Many midwives and doctors suggest having the bag ready between weeks 34 and 36. Since labour can start any time after week 37, it is best not to leave it to the last weeks. If you are at risk of an early birth, your doctor may ask you to prepare sooner.

Ask your hospital in advance what they provide. Some supply nappies, baby clothes and maternity pads, which makes your bag much lighter.

Documents

  • ID for mum and partner
  • Health insurance details
  • Pregnancy notes, test and scan results
  • Your birth plan and any notes from your doctor

For mum

  • 2 front-opening nightdresses or pyjama sets (easier for breastfeeding)
  • A dressing gown and non-slip slippers
  • A nursing bra and breast pads
  • Comfortable high-waisted underwear
  • Maternity pads (if the hospital doesn't provide them)
  • Toothbrush, toothpaste, shampoo, hairbrush, hair ties, lip balm
  • Phone charger with a long cable
  • A water bottle (one with a straw is easier when lying down)
  • Loose, comfortable clothes for going home

For baby

  • 3–4 bodysuits and 2–3 sleepsuits (some newborn size, some 0–3 months)
  • A thin hat and socks
  • A swaddle or light blanket
  • Newborn nappies and wipes (if the hospital doesn't provide them)
  • A going-home outfit and an outer layer for the season
  • An infant car seat, fitted in the car beforehand

For the birth partner

  • A change of clothes
  • Snacks and water
  • Phone charger and a power bank
  • A list of who is waiting for news, and how you will tell them

The most forgotten things

  • The car seat: you need it to take the baby home safely. Practise fitting it before the day.
  • A long charging cable: hospital sockets are often far from the bed.
  • A plan for sharing the news: messaging everyone one by one after the birth is tiring. Sharing the news with one link makes it much easier.
This list is a general guide. Your hospital's and your doctor's advice always comes first.
